Sundial

Two pictures made of mass. The left one has to become the right one, and moving mass costs money in proportion to how far it goes. Finding the cheapest way to do that is called optimal transport, and on a pair of 32×32 grids it's a linear program with 1,048,576 variables: one for every route from a starting square to a destination square.

It solves live on your GPU, as WebGPU compute shaders. No server, no install, no CUDA, and nothing leaves your machine. Every tolerance below is re-verified on the CPU in f64 before it's reported, because the GPU never grades its own homework. Benchmark page →   Taxi demo →

This browser has no WebGPU. Chrome/Edge 113+, Firefox 141+, or Safari 26+ required.

Three measures of how wrong the current answer still is, on a log scale. Primal residual is how much the plan breaks the rules, dual residual is how far the prices are from consistent, and duality gap is how far the cost is from the best possible cost. The dashed line is the tolerance. All three have to get under it, and then the CPU checks the answer again in double precision before it says "Optimal".
